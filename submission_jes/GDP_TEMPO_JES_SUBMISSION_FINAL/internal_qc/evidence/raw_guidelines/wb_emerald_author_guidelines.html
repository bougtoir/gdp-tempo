    Author checklist

    What you need to know

    • Is your paper a good fit for your chosen journal? 
      Check by reading the aims and scope on our journal homepages and look at recently published articles.

    • Have you checked the manuscript requirements for your chosen journal? 
      Find our general manuscript requirements below and refer to your chosen journal’s homepage to check specific manuscript requirements such as article length, reference type and more.

    • Have you read and understood Emerald’s publication ethics guidelines and your author responsibilities? 
      Our publication ethics and author policies apply to all Emerald journals, and all authors should read them before submitting. Please also ensure you refer to our guidelines about permitted uses of AI and Emerald’s full AI policy, including examples of use cases, which can all be found on our Publishing Ethics page.

    Prepare your manuscript

    • Have you checked for spelling or typographical errors? 
      Is your manuscript complete, grammatically correct, and without spelling or typographical errors?

    • Have you cleared any necessary permissions? 
      Find our guidance on permissions below and visit our permissions page for further information.

    • Have you checked for any unintended identifying information? 
      Ensure your manuscript does not contain any information, such as author names, that could reveal your identity in the anonymous peer review process. When citing your own work, use wording like ‘previous research has shown’. Do not include your unpublished work in the reference list. Finally, make sure to upload any acknowledgements or author biographies as separate files.

    • Have you identified appropriate keywords? 
      Check keyword numbers by reading the aims and scope on our journal homepages. Find out how to make your paper more discoverable.

    • Have you written an effective title and abstract? 
      Manuscript titles should be descriptive of the content and contain relevant keywords that will improve the visibility of your research in online databases. Abstracts should follow the structured abstract format. For more information see guidelines below.

    • Have you read through the formatting requirements laid out in these guidelines? 

    If not, then please take the time to read through the guidelines beneath this checklist. 

    Before you submit

    Author responsibilities

    We aim to deliver a professional and courteous experience throughout the publication and review process. Please see our Dignity at Work policy for more information.

    As an author, our expectation is that you will:

    • Respond swiftly to any queries during the publication process.

    • Be accountable for all aspects of your work. This includes investigating and resolving any questions about accuracy or research integrity.

    • Treat communications between you and the journal editor as confidential until an editorial decision has been made.

    Research and publishing ethics

    Our editors and employees work hard to ensure the content we publish is ethically sound. Prior to submission, please ensure you read and adhere to all relevant publishing ethics policies as outlined in our publication ethics guidelines.

    Emerald is a member of the Committee on Publication Ethics(COPE); we abide by COPE’s Principles of Transparency and Best Practice in Scholarly Publishing

    Originality

    Any manuscript you submit should be original. That means it should not have been published before in its current, or similar, form. Failure to comply with this may result in your work being desk-rejected by the journal editorial office. For further guidance please see our editorial policy and originality guidelines. Exceptions to this rule are outlined in our pre-print and conference paper policies. If any substantial element of your paper has been previously published, you need to declare this to the journal editor upon submission. 

    Consent to publish

    If you have written about a company/individual/organisation in detail using information that is not publicly available, have spent time within that company/organisation, or the work features named/interviewed employees, you will need to clear permission by using the consent to publish form. 

    If you have to clear permission with the company/individual/organisation, consent must be given either by the named individual in question or their representative, a board member of the company/organisation, or a HR department representative of the company/organisation. Please also see our permissions guidance for full details.

    Human participants

    You should conduct your research ethically and responsibly. This includes adhering to national and international research ethics guidelines, the ethical principles outlined by your discipline and any relevant authorities, and to be transparent about your research methods. All involved in the publication process should be able to fairly and appropriately evaluate your work. 

    For all research involving human participants, you must ensure that you have obtained informed consent, meaning that you must inform all participants in your work (or their legal representative) as to why the research is being conducted, whether their anonymity is protected, how their data will be stored and used, and whether there are any associated risks from participation in the study. 

    The submitted work must confirm that informed consent was obtained and detail how this was addressed in accordance with our policy on informed consent.

    Ethical statements

    Where appropriate, you must provide an ethical statement within the submitted work confirming that your research received institutional and national (or international) ethical approval, and that it complies with all relevant guidelines and regulations for studies involving humans, whether that be data, individuals, or samples.

    A statement requires:

    • The name and location of the institutional ethics reviewing committee or review board;

    • The approval number and the date of approval;

    • The details of the national or international guidelines that were followed;

    • Any other relevant information.

    You should also include details of how the work adheres to relevant consent guidelines along with confirming that informed consent was secured for all participants. The details of these statements should ensure that author and participant anonymity is not compromised. 

    Any work submitted without a suitable ethical statement and details of informed consent for all participants, where required, will be returned to the authors, and will not be considered further until appropriate and clear documentation is provided. 

    Emerald reserves the right to reject work without sufficient evidence of informed consent from human participants and ethical approval where required.

    Transparency and Openness Promotion (TOP) guidelines

    We are a signatory of the Transparency and Openness Promotion (TOP) Guidelines, a framework that supports the reproducibility of research through the adoption of transparent research practices. That means we encourage you to:

    • Cite and fully reference all data, program code, and other methods in your article.

    • Include persistent identifiers, such as a Digital Object Identifier (DOI), in references for datasets and program codes. Persistent identifiers ensure future access to unique published digital objects, such as a piece of text or datasets. Persistent identifiers are assigned to datasets by digital archives, such as institutional repositories and partners in the Data Preservation Alliance for the Social Sciences (Data-PASS).

    • Follow appropriate international and national procedures with respect to data protection, rights to privacy and other ethical considerations, whenever you cite data.

    • For an example on how to cite databases, please refer to the references section below.

    • For further guidance, please refer to our research and publishing ethics guidelines.

    Copyright and Third-Party Permissions

    By submitting your work to Emerald, you are guaranteeing that the work is not in infringement of any existing copyright.

    Prior to article submission, you need to ensure you have applied for, and received, written permission to use any material in your manuscript that has been created by a third party. We are unable to publish any article that still has permissions pending. Please read our guide to publishing permissions to ensure you have met all the requirements, so that we can process your submission without delay.

    Open Access

    All of our journals currently offer two open access (OA) publishing routes: gold open access and green open access (self-archiving).

    Gold open access:

    To make the branded publisher PDF (the version of record) freely available upon publication, authors can opt for the gold open access route. Authors can publish via the gold open access route in our fully open access journals, or in our hybrid journals. Following the acceptance of your article it will pass to our internal licencing system. If you would like to publish your article open access please ensure you select the open access option at this point.

    If you choose to publish gold open access, you will be asked to pay an APC (article processing charge), which varies by journal and can be found on our APC price list or on the editorial system at the point of submission. 

    A number of organisations have established partnerships with us to cover the cost of an agreed number of gold open access articles or agreed a discount for their regions. As an author, if you are affiliated with one of our partner organisations you may be eligible for a prepaid open access publishing voucher. To check your eligibility please refer to our list of open access agreements.

    For further guidance on APCs and payments please refer to our publication charges guidelines.

    Your article will be published with a Creative Commons CC BY 4.0 user licence, which outlines how readers can reuse your work.

    Green open access:

    If you would like to, or are required to, publish open access but do not have the funding to cover the APC, you can choose the green open access, or self-archiving, route. Once your article is published, you can immediately make the author-accepted-manuscript (the version accepted for publication) openly available, free from payment and embargo periods.

    For UK journal article authors 

    If you wish to submit your work accepted by Emerald to REF 2029, you must make a ‘closed deposit’ of your accepted manuscript to your respective institutional repository upon acceptance of your article. Articles accepted for publication after 1st January 2021 should be deposited as soon as possible, but no later than three months after the acceptance date. For further information and guidance, please refer to the REF 2029 website. Emerald is one of only a handful of publishers that doesn’t impose an embargo period. You may deposit your author-accepted-manuscript at any point, but it must not be made publicly accessible until official publication (i.e. as soon as it appears on Emerald Insight in its final typeset version).

    Prepare your manuscript

    Manuscript requirements

    Before you submit your manuscript, it’s important you read and follow the guidelines below. You will also find some useful tips in our structure your journal submission how-to guide.

    Our partners, Editage, offer a range of services specially designed to help authors through every stage of their academic journey and offer assistance in language editing, translation, formatting and other services.

    File format

    Article files should be provided in a Microsoft word format.

    While you can submit a PDF of the document alongside the Word file, PDFs alone are not acceptable. LaTeX files are accepted only if an accompanying PDF document is provided. Acceptable figure file types are listed further below.

    Title

    A concisely worded title should be provided.

    Author details and biographies

    The names of all contributing authors should be added to the ScholarOne submission. Please list them in the order in which you would like them to be published.

    Each contributing author needs their own ScholarOne author account, which include the following details:

    • Author email address (institutional preferred).

    • Author name (we will reproduce it exactly, so any middle names and/or initials they want featured must be included.

    • Author affiliation (this should be where they were based when the research was conducted).

    Read about our research ethics for authorship. For multi-authored work, ALL authors who have made a significant contribution should be listed. You should not include people who have not contributed to the work or who do not want to be associated with the research. Those who provided support but did not contribute to the research manuscript should feature in an acknowledgements section. 

    If you wish to include author biographies and/or acknowledgements, save them in a separate Microsoft Word document and upload the file with your submission. Where they are included, a brief professional biography of not more than 100 words should be supplied for each named author.

    Research funding

    Your article must reference all sources of external research funding in the acknowledgements section. You should describe the role of the funder or financial sponsor in the entire research process, from study design to submission.

    Structured abstract

    All articles must include a structured abstract, following this format. You can find some useful tips in our write an article abstract how-to guide.

    These four sub-headings and their accompanying explanations must always be included:

    • Purpose

    • Design/methodology/approach

    • Findings

    • Originality

    The below sub-headings are optional and can be included, if applicable:

    • Research limitations/implications

    • Practical implications

    • Social implications

    The maximum length of your abstract should be 250 words in total, including keywords and article classification (see the sections below).

    Article keywords

    Your submission should include up to 6 appropriate and short keywords that capture the key topics of the paper. Choosing the right key words can help enhance the online discoverability of your research and may also be used to help find relevant reviewers. 

    Our in-house editorial team may replace some of them with matching terms to ensure consistency across publications and enhance your article’s visibility.

    Article classification

    Each individual journal homepage will list the type of manuscripts the journal publishes along with guidance on word count.

    When submitting your article in ScholarOne, you will also be asked to select the manuscript category for your paper. Manuscript categories are used to determine how content is displayed on Emerald Insight, Emerald’s content platform. The following is a list of typical manuscript categories.

    • Research Paper: Covers papers which report on any type of research undertaken by the author(s). The research may involve the construction or testing of a model or framework, action research, testing of data, market research or surveys, empirical, scientific or clinical research.

    • Case Report: Describes actual interventions or experiences within organizations. They may well be subjective and will not generally report on research. A description of a legal case or a hypothetical case study used as a teaching exercise would also fit into this category.

    • Practitioner Paper: Describes and/or evaluates technical products, processes or services, focuses on issues related to bridging research and practice, or discusses challenges in workplace management from a practice or policy perspective.

    • Technical Paper: A brief article describing a pretrial feasibility or pilot efficacy study that addresses important clinical questions. It might also include recommendations and conclusions of the research.

    • Conceptual Paper: Papers not based on research but will develop hypotheses. The papers are likely to be discursive and will cover philosophical discussions and comparative studies of others' work and thinking.

    • Literature Review: A survey of articles, books, and other sources relevant to a particular issue, field of research, or theory. It collects key sources on a topic and discusses those sources in conversation with each other (also called synthesis).

    • Viewpoint: Usually written by practitioners emphasising an opinion, explanation or novel ideas about the future of the subject. These papers will be reviewed by a member of the editorial board in such a way as to offer authors guidance for improvement.

    • General Review: Synthesizes and discusses established knowledge, frameworks, or historical developments. General reviews provide integrative and descriptive overviews intended to build understanding of a concept, phenomenon, or methodological tradition, and may include instructional elements such as models, tools, or approaches used in practice or research.

    • Impact Article: Facilitates knowledge exchange about impact, namely, how to collaborate on, design, develop, and demonstrate impact. Impact articles are typically written not upon completion of research but at the point of, or after, the actual change (or changes) which has resulted from it.

    • Brief Report: A brief and concise overview of recent research in a specific field well in the scope of this Journal and supported by bibliography belonging to quality sources.

    • Extended Editorial: Provide original insights as an introduction to a special issue or as a piece for a standard issue from the editorial team.

    • Editorial: Introduction to an issue written by the journal editor or guest editor. An editorial is not double blind refereed.

    • Book Review: Aims at providing insight and opinion on recently published scholarly books. Such submissions are short articles introducing new literature in the field.

    • Guest Editorial: Editorial for special issues that presents aims of contributing articles, as well as placing issue in broader context. Written by the guest editor for a specific journal issue.

    • Executive Summary: Provides a concise, accessible overview of key findings, implications, and contributions of a larger research report or initiative relevant to gender and entrepreneurship. Written to support rapid understanding among practitioners, policymakers, and non-academic audiences. 

    • Discussion: Offer a focused analysis of an emerging issue, debate, or methodological concern in gender and entrepreneurship. They aim to provoke reflection, clarify tensions, or outline research and policy implications. These papers are typically invited or editor-approved submissions.

    • Precis: Typically commissioned by the editorial team. A precis article is a summary of an original article which has been previously published by Emerald.

    Please see the journal landing page for the specific kinds of papers accepted by the journal, as well as guidance on maximum word counts. 

    Headings

    Headings must be concise, with a clear indication of the required hierarchy. The preferred format is for first level headings to be in bold, and subsequent sub-headings to be in mediumitalics.

    Notes and endnotes

    Notes or endnotes should only be used if absolutely necessary. They should be identified in the text by consecutive numbers enclosed in square brackets. These numbers should then be listed, and explained, at the end of the article.

    Figures

    If you are planning to use figures in your submission, here is some best practice:

    • All figures (charts, diagrams, line drawings, webpages/screenshots, and photographic images) should be submitted electronically. Colour and black and white files are accepted.

    • Acceptable formats are .ai, .eps, .jpeg, .bmp, and .tif.

    • Electronic figures created in other applications should be supplied in their original formats and should also be either copied and pasted into a blank Microsoft Word document or submitted as a PDF file.

    • All figures should be supplied at highest resolution/quality possible with numbers and text clearly legible.

    • All figures should be numbered consecutively with Arabic numerals (digits 0-9) and have clear captions.

    • All photographs should be numbered as Plate 1, 2, 3, etc. and have clear captions.

    • All figure/table captions should include the necessary credit line, acknowledgement, or attribution if you have been given permission to use the figure/table. If the figure/table is the property of the author(s), this should be acknowledged in the caption.

    • The submission and publication of images created by AI tools or large-scale generative models is subject to their intended purpose and potential rights requirements. Purpose is here defined as either illustrative, which is permitted, or factual/evidential, which is not permitted. Any such usage must not breach Emerald’s plagiarism policy. For full details, see Emerald’s AI policy.

    Tables

    If you are planning to use tables in your submission, here is some best practice:

    • Tables should be typed and submitted in a separate file to the main body of the article. 

    • The position of each table should be clearly labelled in the main body of the article with corresponding labels clearly shown in the table file. 

    • Tables should be numbered consecutively in Roman numerals (e.g. I, II, etc.).

    • Give each table a main title.

    • Tables should include column headings that signpost the information contained in the columns.

    • Ensure that any superscripts or asteriss are shown next to the relevant items and have explanations displayed as footnotes to the table, figure or plate.

    Supplementary files

    Where tables, figures, appendices, and other additional content are supplementary to the article but not critical to a reader’s understanding of it, you can choose to publish these supplementary files alongside your article on Emerald Insight, our content-hosting platform or on an alternative trusted online repository. 

    All supplementary material must be submitted prior to acceptance. Extensive supplementary material may be subject to peer review at the editor’s discretion. It may be dependent on the content of the material (e.g. whether including it would support the reviewer making a decision on the article during peer review).

    We recommend using Emerald Insight to host your files as we can ensure your supplementary material remains accessible.

    Hosting your files on Emerald Insight

    If you choose to host your supplementary files on Emerald Insight, please submit these as separate files alongside your article.

    Emerald Insight can host the following file types and extensions:

    • Adobe Acrobat (.pdf)

    • Microsoft Word document (.doc, .docx)

    • Microsoft Excel (.xls, xlsx)

    • Microsoft PowerPoint (.pptx)

    • Image (.png, .jpeg, .gif)

    • Plain ASCII text (.txt)

    • PostScript (.ps)

    • Rich Text Format (.rtf)

    Files should be clearly labelled to indicate they are supplementary (e.g. ‘Supplementary_material_appendix_1’ or ‘Supplementary tables’).

    All supplementary material must be mentioned at the appropriate moment in the main text of the article. There is no need to include the content of the file, only the file name.

    A link to the supplementary material will be added to the article during production, and the material will be made available alongside the main article text at the point of EarlyCite publication.

    Emerald will not make changes to the material; it will not be copy-edited or typeset, and authors will not receive proofs of this content.

    Emerald strongly recommends that you style all supplementary material ahead of acceptance of the article.

    Hosting your files on a trusted repository

    Emerald otherwise recommends that authors use the following two lists when searching for a trusted repository:

    • https://commons.datacite.org/repositories

    • https://www.re3data.org

    It is the responsibility of the submitting author to ensure that the material is free to access and that it remains permanently available.

    If using an alternative trusted online repository, you should ensure that the supplementary material is hosted on the repository ahead of submission and then include a link to the repository within the article. The files should be presented as read-only. 

    Please be aware that you risk compromising your anonymity during the review process if the repository contains any information that may enable the reviewer to identify you. Please review any links to alternative repositories carefully prior to submission.

    Referencing

    All references in your manuscript should be formatted using the journal's preferred reference style (please refer to the journal homepage) which will be either Harvard or APA. As the majority of our journals use the Harvard reference style, guidelines for this are provided below. For guidance on the APA reference style please refer to the APA style guide.

    Please ensure you check all your citations for completeness, accuracy, and consistency.

    Emerald’s Harvard referencing style:

    References to other publications in your text should be written as follows:

    • Single author: (Adams, 2006)

    • Two authors: (Adams and Brown, 2006)

    • Three or more authors: (Adams et al., 2006) – note that ‘et al’ should be italicised.

    When referring to pages in a publication, use ‘p. [page number]’ for a single page or ‘pp. [page numbers]’ to indicate a page range. Page numbers should be written out in full, e.g. 175-179, not 175-9.

    Where a colon or dash appears in the title of an article or book chapter, the letter that follows that colon or dash should always be lower case.

    When citing a work with multiple editors, use the abbreviation ‘Ed.s’.

    At the end of your paper, please supply a reference list in alphabetical order using the style guidelines below. Where a DOI is available, this should be included at the end of the reference.

    For journals

    Surname, initials (year), "title of article", journal name, volume issue, page numbers.

    e.g. Capizzi, M.T. and Ferguson, R. (2005), "Loyalty trends for the twenty-first century", Journal of Consumer Marketing, Vol. 22 No. 2, pp.72-80.

    For books

    Surname, initials (year), title of book, publisher, place of publication.

    e.g. Harrow, R. (2005), No Place to Hide, Simon & Schuster, New York, NY.

    For book chapters

    Surname, initials (year), "chapter title", editor's surname, initials (Ed.), title of book, publisher, place of publication, page numbers.

    e.g. Calabrese, F.A. (2005), "The early pathways: theory to practice – a continuum", Stankosky, M. (Ed.), Creating the Discipline of Knowledge Management, Elsevier, New York, NY, pp.15-20.

    For published conference proceedings

    Surname, initials (year of publication), "title of paper", in editor’s surname, initials (Ed.), title of published proceeding which may include place and date(s) held, publisher, place of publication, page numbers.

    e.g. Wilde, S. and Cox, C. (2008), “Principal factors contributing to the competitiveness of tourism destinations at varying stages of development”, in Richardson, S., Fredline, L., Patiar A., & Ternel, M. (Ed.s), CAUTHE 2008: Where the 'bloody hell' are we?, Griffith University, Gold Coast, Qld, pp.115-118.

    For unpublished conference proceedings

    Surname, initials (year), "title of paper", paper presented at [name of conference], [date of conference], [place of conference], available at: URL if freely available on the internet (accessed date).

    e.g. Aumueller, D. (2005), "Semantic authoring and retrieval within a wiki", paper presented at the European Semantic Web Conference (ESWC), 29 May-1 June, Heraklion, Crete, available at: http://dbs.uni-leipzig.de/file/aumueller05wiksar.pdf (accessed 20 February 2007).

    For working papers

    Surname, initials (year), "title of article", working paper [number if available], institution or organization, place of organization, date.

    e.g. Moizer, P. (2003), "How published academic research can inform policy decisions: the case of mandatory rotation of audit appointments", working paper, Leeds University Business School, University of Leeds, Leeds, 28 March.

    For encyclopaedia entries (with no author or editor)

    Title of encyclopaedia (year), "title of entry", volume, edition, title of encyclopaedia, publisher, place of publication, page numbers.

    e.g. Encyclopaedia Britannica (1926), "Psychology of culture contact", Vol. 1, 13th ed., Encyclopaedia Britannica, London and New York, NY, pp.765-771.

    (for authored entries, please refer to book chapter guidelines above)

    For newspaper articles (authored):

    Surname, initials (year), "article title", newspaper, date, page numbers.

    e.g. Smith, A. (2008), "Money for old rope", Daily News, 21 January, pp.1, 3-4.

    For newspaper articles (non-authored):

    Newspaper (year), "article title", date, page numbers.

    e.g. Daily News (2008), "Small change", 2 February, p.7.

    For archival or other unpublished sources:

    Surname, initials (year), "title of document", unpublished manuscript, collection name, inventory record, name of archive, location of archive.

    e.g. Litman, S. (1902), "Mechanism & Technique of Commerce", unpublished manuscript, Simon Litman Papers, Record series 9/5/29 Box 3, University of Illinois Archives, Urbana-Champaign, IL.

    For electronic sources

    If available online, the full URL should be supplied at the end of the reference, as well as the date that the resource was accessed.

    Surname, initials (year), “title of electronic source”, available at: persistent URL (accessed date month year).

    e.g. Weida, S. and Stolley, K. (2013), “Developing strong thesis statements”, available at: https://owl.english.purdue.edu/owl/resource/588/1/ (accessed 20 June 2018)

    Standalone URLs, i.e. those without an author or date, should be included either inside parentheses within the main text, or preferably set as a note (Roman numeral within square brackets within text followed by the full URL address at the end of the paper).

    For data

    Surname, initials (year), title of dataset, name of data repository, available at: persistent URL, (accessed date month year).

    e.g. Campbell, A. and Kahn, R.L. (2015), American National Election Study, 1948, ICPSR07218-v4, Inter-university Consortium for Political and Social Research (distributor), Ann Arbor, MI, available at: https://doi.org/10.3886/ICPSR07218.v4 (accessed 20 June 2018)

    Submit your manuscript

    There are some key steps you should follow to ensure a trouble-free submission.

    The submission process

    All manuscripts should be submitted through our online editorial system, ScholarOne.

    ScholarOne

    You can only submit to the journal through the journal’s ScholarOne site as accessed via the Emerald website. Submissions should be done directly by the author(s) through the ScholarOne site and not via email, a journal representative, or third-party agent/company/journal proxy on their behalf.

    A separate author account is required for each journal you submit to. If this is your first time submitting to this journal, please choose the Create an account or Register now option in the editorial system. If you already have an Emerald login, you are welcome to reuse the existing username and password here.

    The next time you log into the system, you will be asked for your username. This will be the email address you entered when you set up your account.

    Visit the ScholarOne support centre for further help and guidance.

    ORCiDID

    Ensure you add your ORCiD ID during the submission process so it can be embedded in your published article, along with a link to the ORCiD registry. This allows others to easily match you with your work.

    Don’t have one yet? It only takes a few moments to register for a free ORCiD identifier.

    What you can expect next

    You will receive an automated email from the journal editor confirming your successful submission. It will provide you with a manuscript ID, which will be used in all future correspondence about your submission. If you have any reason to suspect the confirmation you received might be fraudulent, please contact the relevant journal editor in the first instance.

    The Peer Review process

    Review and decision process

    Upon submission, each manuscript is screened and checked by the journal’s editorial office. At this stage, they may choose to reject your manuscript if it does not match the journal’s aims and scope or they feel the quality is not ready to be considered for publication. The editorial team may also choose to return the manuscript to you if it does not fit the journal’s submission policies, such as wordcount, anonymity requirements, etc for you to fix prior to peer review. 

    If they think the manuscript is suitable for the journal, the editorial team will send it to at least two independent referees for double-anonymous peer review. Based on their feedback, the editor may accept your manuscript, request minor or major revisions, or decline your work. For further information please refer to our guidance on the peer review process.

    Editors aim to inform you of their first decision within 80 days, though all journals work to different timescales. Further information on the peer review turnaround times for each journal can be found on the journal’s homepage. For updates on the status of your manuscript please refer to the author centre in your ScholarOne account. 

    Each communication from ScholarOne will include the manuscript ID you were given at the point of submission. If you receive an email that does not match these criteria, it could be fraudulent, and we recommend you contact the journal editor in the first instance.

    Manuscript transfer service

    Emerald’s Manuscript Transfer Service takes the pain out of the submission process if your manuscript doesn’t fit your initial journal choice. Our team of expert editors from participating journals work together to identify alternative journals that better align with your research, ensuring your work finds the ideal publication home it deserves. Our dedicated team is committed to supporting authors like you in finding the right home for your research.

    If a journal is participating in the manuscript transfer service, the editor has the option to recommend your paper for transfer. If a transfer decision is made by the editor, you will receive an email with the details of the recommended journal and the option to accept or reject the transfer. It’s always down to you as the author to decide if you’d like to accept. If you do accept, your paper and any reviewer reports will automatically be transferred to the recommended journals. Authors will then confirm resubmissions in the new journal’s ScholarOne system.

    Our Manuscript Transfer Service page has more information on the process.

    If your submission is accepted

    Copyright

    All accepted authors are sent an email with a link to a copyright licence form. Please check this for accuracy, especially concerning affiliation and contact details and the spelling of your name, and return it to us electronically. 

    If there is a reason why you can’t assign copyright to us, you should discuss this with your journal editorial office contact. You will find their contact details in the journal’s editorial team section.

    If at this stage you opt to publish your work via the gold open access route, your work will be published with a Creative Commons CC BY 4.0 user licence.

    Proofing and typesetting

    Once we have received your completed licence form, the article will pass directly into the production process. We will carry out editorial checks, copyediting, and typesetting and then return proofs to you (if you are the corresponding author) for your review. This is your opportunity to correct any typographical errors, grammatical errors, or incorrect author details. We can’t accept requests to rewrite texts at this stage.

    When the page proofs are finalised, the fully typeset and proofed version of record is published online. This is referred to as the EarlyCite version. While an EarlyCite article has yet to be assigned to a volume or issue, it does have a digital object identifier (DOI) and is fully citable. It will be compiled into an issue according to the journal’s issue schedule, with papers being added by chronological date of publication.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is my paper suitable for the journal?

    If you’ve read the journal’s aims and scope and are still unsure whether your paper is suitable for the journal, please email the editor and include your paper's title and structured abstract. They will be able to advise on your manuscript’s suitability. You will find their contact details on the editorial team tab on this page.

    Is there a submission fee for the journal?

    The only time we will ever ask you for money to publish in an Emerald journal is if you have chosen to publish via the gold open access route. You will be asked to pay an APC (article-processing charge) once your paper has been accepted (unless it is a sponsored open access journal), and never at submission.

    At no other time will you be asked to contribute financially towards your article’s publication, processing, or review. 

    If you haven’t chosen gold open access and you receive an email that appears to be from Emerald, the journal, or a third party, asking you for payment to publish, please contact our support team via [email protected].

    Who do I contact if I have a query about my submission?

    Please email the journal editor – you will find their contact details on the journal landing page. If you ever suspect an email you’ve received from Emerald might not be genuine, you can verify it with the commissioning editor for the journal, whose contact details can be found on the journal landing page.

    Who do I contact if I want to find out which volume and issue my accepted paper will appear in?

    Typically, papers are added to an issue according to their date of publication. Once your paper has been published in an issue, you will be notified by email.

    How do I make a change to the list of authors once the manuscript has been submitted?

    Authorship and the order in which the authors are listed on the paper should be agreed prior to submission. We have a right-first-time policy on this, and no changes can be made to the list once submitted. If you have made an error in the submission process, please email the journal editorial office who will look into your request – you will find their contact details on the journal landing page.

    How can I become a reviewer for the journal?

    Please contact the editor for the journal, with a copy of your CV. You will find their contact details on the journal landing page. Please also see our guide on becoming a peer reviewer.
